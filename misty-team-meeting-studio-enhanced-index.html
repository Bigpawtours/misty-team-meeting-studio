KEEPING IT TOGETHER

Same house. Different information. One transaction to keep together.

BUYER vs. SELLER
Hosted byMisty Borhart
01 • MEET THE HOUSE

What concerns you?

Based only on what you just saw. Don't debate the answers yet.

Inspection was yesterday. The report just came in.
02 • THE TRANSACTION
$572K

You're under contract.

3 bedrooms • 2 bathrooms • 1,470 sf • Built 1972 • Conventional financing

Inspection contingency ends tomorrow at 5:00 PM.
03 • CHOOSE YOUR SIDE

Buyer Team vs. Seller Team

BUYER

Inspection report + confidential buyer instructions. Decide what matters and how you'll position it.

SELLER

Disclosure/history + confidential seller instructions. Decide what you defend, concede or challenge.

Do not share your packet with the other side.

04 • HUDDLE
7:00

Prepare your client. Prepare your position.

What are you advising your client? What's your opening position? What actually matters enough to jeopardize the transaction?

05 • LIVE NEGOTIATION

Have the conversation.

One Buyer Agent. One Listing Agent. Everyone else listens.

Don't tell us what you would say. Say it.
06 • FREEZE

New information
just arrived.

A licensed plumber reviewed the sewer scope. The line is functioning, but repair is recommended within approximately 12 months. Written estimate: $7,400. The plumber does not characterize it as an emergency.

Two minutes with your team. Then one final negotiation.

07 • FINAL POSITION

What matters enough
to risk the deal?

Come back with your best path forward. Agreement is optional. Sound client advice is not.

08 • THE REVEAL

What did the other side know?

BUYER

They love the house, lost two prior offers and have about $12,000 of accessible savings after closing. Sewer + moisture are the true priorities.

SELLER

They already bought their next home, need this closing, and privately authorized up to $8,000 in concessions. They prefer a credit.

09 • DEBRIEF

Knowing that…
what changes?

What moved the negotiation forward? What weakened it? What did each agent need to do with their own client before they negotiated?

The report is not the negotiation.
10 • CLOSE THE LOOP

Did we keep it together?

Choose the outcome, then talk about whether the advice was sound — not just whether the transaction survived.
